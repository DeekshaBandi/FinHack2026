# Climate Risk Framework for Portfolio Management

## TCFD Framework Overview

The Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) provides a framework for understanding climate risks in investment portfolios.

### Two Categories of Climate Risk:

## 1. Physical Risk

Physical risks result from climate change and can be:

### Acute Physical Risks:
- **Hurricanes/Cyclones**: Wind damage, storm surge, flooding
- **Wildfires**: Direct property damage, smoke damage
- **Floods**: Riverine flooding, flash floods, coastal flooding
- **Extreme Heat**: Equipment failures, worker productivity
- **Extreme Cold**: Freeze events, infrastructure damage
- **Droughts**: Water scarcity, agricultural impacts

### Chronic Physical Risks:
- **Sea Level Rise**: Coastal property inundation
- **Changing Precipitation Patterns**: Water availability
- **Rising Mean Temperatures**: Cooling costs, crop yields
- **Ocean Acidification**: Marine industry impacts

## 2. Transition Risk

Transition risks arise from the shift to a lower-carbon economy:

### Policy and Legal Risk:
- Carbon taxes and cap-and-trade systems
- Emissions regulations
- Climate litigation
- Stranded asset write-downs

### Technology Risk:
- Disruption from clean energy technologies
- Electric vehicle adoption
- Energy storage breakthroughs
- Green hydrogen development

### Market Risk:
- Shifting consumer preferences
- Commodity price changes
- Investor divestment campaigns
- Supply chain decarbonization requirements

### Reputation Risk:
- Stakeholder pressure on high-emitting companies
- Greenwashing accusations
- Social license to operate

## Climate VaR (Value at Risk)

### Definition:
Climate VaR estimates the potential loss in portfolio value due to climate-related risks at a specified confidence level (typically 95th percentile).

### Components:
1. **Physical Risk VaR**: Losses from direct climate impacts
2. **Transition Risk VaR**: Losses from policy/market shifts
3. **Contagion Risk VaR**: Indirect losses through supply chains

### Calculation Approach:
```
Physical VaR = Σ (Asset Value × Physical Exposure × Loss Severity × Probability)
Transition VaR = Σ (Asset Value × Carbon Intensity × Carbon Price × Duration)
Contagion VaR = (Physical + Transition VaR) × Supply Chain Factor
Total Climate VaR = Physical + Transition + Contagion VaR
```

## Scenario Analysis

### NGFS Scenarios (Network for Greening the Financial System):

**1. Orderly Transition**
- Early, smooth policy action
- Gradual increase in carbon prices
- Lower physical risk (1.5-2°C warming)
- Lower transition risk (predictable policy)

**2. Disorderly Transition**
- Delayed, sudden policy action
- Sharp carbon price increases
- Higher transition risk (policy shock)
- Moderate physical risk

**3. Hot House World**
- No additional policy action
- High physical risk (3-4°C warming)
- Low transition risk (no policy change)
- Catastrophic long-term outcomes

### Stress Test Scenarios:
- Category 5 Hurricane (Gulf Coast)
- California Megafire
- Texas Grid Failure (Freeze/Heat)
- $150/ton Carbon Tax
- Compound Event (Multiple simultaneous disasters)

## Portfolio Management Implications

### Exposure Assessment:
1. Map holdings to physical locations
2. Assess sector-level transition exposure
3. Identify supply chain dependencies
4. Calculate aggregate portfolio exposure

### Hedging Strategies:
1. **Direct Hedges**: Short high-exposure stocks
2. **Sector Hedges**: Short energy ETFs
3. **Insurance**: Catastrophe bonds, weather derivatives
4. **Diversification**: Geographic and sector spread

### Rebalancing Triggers:
- Physical risk score exceeds threshold
- Carbon intensity above peer average
- Concentration in single hazard zone
- Event-driven risk spike

## Regulatory Landscape

### Current Requirements:
- SEC Climate Disclosure Rules (pending)
- EU Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR)
- UK Climate Financial Risk Forum guidance
- California Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act

### Emerging Standards:
- ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board)
- Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)
- Transition Pathway Initiative (TPI)
- Climate Action 100+ engagement

## Industry-Specific Considerations

### Energy Sector:
- Highest physical risk (coastal infrastructure)
- Highest transition risk (stranded assets)
- Most impacted by carbon pricing
- Potential winners: Renewables, clean tech

### Utilities:
- Grid vulnerability to extreme weather
- Regulatory rate recovery mechanisms
- Renewable energy transition opportunities
- Water availability concerns

### Real Estate:
- Direct physical asset exposure
- Flood/hurricane zone mapping
- Building code and resilience requirements
- Green building certification benefits

### Insurance:
- First-order climate risk bearer
- Claim payouts increase with disasters
- Repricing and withdrawal from markets
- Reinsurance market dynamics
