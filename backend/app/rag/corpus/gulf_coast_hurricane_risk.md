# Gulf Coast Hurricane Risk Analysis

## Geographic Overview

The Gulf Coast region spans from Texas through Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama, and the Florida Panhandle. This region faces the highest hurricane exposure in the continental United States.

### Hurricane Frequency:
- Major hurricanes (Category 3+): Average 1.5 per year make U.S. landfall
- Gulf Coast receives ~60% of all U.S. hurricane landfalls
- Peak season: August - October
- Extended season: June - November

## Critical Infrastructure Exposure

### Energy Infrastructure:
The Gulf Coast contains the highest concentration of U.S. energy infrastructure:

**Refineries**:
- ~50% of U.S. refining capacity located in Gulf Coast
- Major facilities: Port Arthur, Houston, Beaumont, Lake Charles
- Combined capacity: 8+ million barrels per day
- Companies: Valero, ExxonMobil, Chevron, Phillips 66, Marathon

**Offshore Production**:
- Gulf of Mexico produces ~15% of U.S. crude oil
- ~5% of U.S. natural gas production
- Thousands of offshore platforms
- Underwater pipelines vulnerable to storm damage

**Petrochemical Facilities**:
- Houston Ship Channel: Largest petrochemical complex
- Companies: LyondellBasell, Dow, BASF, ExxonMobil Chemical
- Products: Plastics, fertilizers, chemicals

### Ports and Shipping:
- Port of Houston: Largest U.S. port by tonnage
- Port of New Orleans: Mississippi River access
- Port of Mobile: Container and bulk shipping
- Storm surge can shut ports for days/weeks

## Historical Hurricane Impacts

### Hurricane Harvey (2017):
- Category 4 at landfall
- 60+ inches of rainfall (record)
- $125 billion in damages (costliest)
- 30% of U.S. refining offline at peak
- Gasoline prices spiked nationwide
- Recovery: 6+ months for full operations

### Hurricane Laura (2020):
- Category 4 at landfall (Lake Charles, LA)
- $19 billion in damages
- Significant industrial damage
- Cameron LNG facility impacted

### Hurricane Ida (2021):
- Category 4 at landfall (Louisiana)
- $75 billion in damages
- All Louisiana refineries shut down
- Power outages lasted weeks
- Oil production shut for months

### Hurricane Beryl (2024):
- Early season storm (July)
- Houston area impact
- Widespread power outages
- Refinery disruptions

## Financial Impact Framework

### Direct Costs:
1. **Physical Damage**: Facility repairs, equipment replacement
2. **Business Interruption**: Lost production during shutdown
3. **Emergency Response**: Evacuation, securing facilities
4. **Restoration**: Returning to full operations

### Indirect Costs:
1. **Supply Chain Disruption**: Downstream customer impacts
2. **Price Volatility**: Commodity price spikes
3. **Insurance Premiums**: Post-event increases
4. **Regulatory Scrutiny**: Safety investigations

### Market Impact:
- Energy stocks typically drop 5-15% on major landfalls
- Refiners see margin expansion post-storm (reduced supply)
- Insurers face claim payouts (stock drops)
- Building materials, generators see demand spike

## Scenario Analysis: Category 5 Gulf Coast Hurricane

### Assumptions:
- Landfall near Houston/Galveston
- Maximum sustained winds: 160 mph
- Storm surge: 15-20 feet
- Rainfall: 30+ inches
- Duration of impact: 48-72 hours

### Energy Sector Impact:
- **Refining**: 40-60% of Gulf Coast capacity offline
- **Duration**: 30-90 days for full restoration
- **Revenue Loss**: $50-100 billion industry-wide
- **Individual Company Losses**: $2-10 billion each

### Portfolio Impact (Hypothetical $1B Portfolio):
If portfolio has 20% Gulf Coast energy exposure:
- Direct equity loss: $30-60M (15-30% drawdown on exposed names)
- Short hedge gains: $15-25M (if hedged with insurers, shorts)
- Net portfolio impact: -$5M to -$35M

## Risk Mitigation Strategies

### For Energy Companies:
1. Facility hardening and elevation
2. Redundant supply routes
3. Emergency response planning
4. Insurance and reinsurance
5. Geographic diversification

### For Investors:
1. Monitor National Hurricane Center forecasts
2. Reduce Gulf Coast exposure before peak season
3. Hedge with insurance company shorts
4. Consider weather derivatives
5. Maintain liquidity for opportunistic buying

## Supply Chain Contagion Paths

### Primary Contagion (Direct Exposure):
```
Hurricane → Refinery Damage → Valero, ExxonMobil, Chevron
```

### Secondary Contagion (Suppliers):
```
Refinery Shutdown → Oilfield Services Demand Drop → Halliburton, Schlumberger
```

### Tertiary Contagion (Downstream):
```
Refined Product Shortage → Petrochemical Feedstock Shortage → Dow, LyondellBasell
```

### Quaternary Contagion (End Products):
```
Chemical Shortage → Packaging Materials → Consumer Goods → Retail
```

## Key Monitoring Indicators

### Pre-Season:
- NOAA hurricane season forecast
- Sea surface temperatures
- El Niño/La Niña conditions
- Company preparedness announcements

### During Season:
- National Hurricane Center advisories
- Tropical storm development
- Forecast track and intensity
- Company evacuation announcements

### Post-Event:
- Damage assessments
- Restart timelines
- Insurance claim estimates
- Regulatory responses
