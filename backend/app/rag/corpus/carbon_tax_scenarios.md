# Carbon Tax Scenario Analysis

## Overview

A carbon tax places a direct price on greenhouse gas emissions, typically measured in dollars per metric ton of CO2 equivalent. This analysis examines portfolio impacts under various carbon pricing scenarios.

## Current Global Carbon Pricing

### Existing Carbon Prices (2026):
- **EU ETS**: ~€80-100/ton (~$90-110)
- **California Cap-and-Trade**: ~$35/ton
- **Canada Federal Carbon Tax**: C$80/ton (~$60)
- **China ETS**: ~$10/ton (limited coverage)
- **UK ETS**: ~£80/ton (~$100)

### U.S. Federal Carbon Tax:
- No federal carbon tax currently
- Various proposals range from $25-150/ton
- Border adjustment mechanisms under discussion

## Scenario Framework

### Scenario 1: Moderate Carbon Tax ($50/ton)
**Assumptions**:
- Phased in over 5 years
- Applies to Scope 1 emissions
- Revenue recycled to households
- Border adjustments included

**Sector Impacts**:
| Sector | Emissions (tons/$M revenue) | Cost Impact |
|--------|----------------------------|-------------|
| Oil & Gas | 150-300 | -15% to -25% EBITDA |
| Utilities | 100-200 | -10% to -20% EBITDA |
| Materials | 50-150 | -8% to -15% EBITDA |
| Industrials | 20-50 | -3% to -8% EBITDA |
| Technology | 5-15 | -1% to -3% EBITDA |

### Scenario 2: Aggressive Carbon Tax ($100/ton)
**Assumptions**:
- Implemented within 3 years
- Applies to Scope 1 and 2 emissions
- Limited revenue recycling
- Strong border adjustments

**Sector Impacts**:
| Sector | Cost Impact | Valuation Impact |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| Oil & Gas | -25% to -40% EBITDA | -30% to -50% |
| Utilities | -15% to -30% EBITDA | -15% to -25% |
| Materials | -12% to -25% EBITDA | -15% to -30% |
| Industrials | -5% to -12% EBITDA | -8% to -15% |
| Financials | -2% to -5% EBITDA | -3% to -8% |

### Scenario 3: Carbon Border Adjustment
**Assumptions**:
- $75/ton price
- Applied to imports based on embedded carbon
- Domestic production exempt if compliant
- Phased implementation

**Trade Flow Impacts**:
- Imported steel/aluminum face tariffs
- Domestic production becomes competitive
- Supply chains may reshort
- Inflation pressure on consumer goods

## Company-Specific Analysis

### Valero Energy (VLO)
**Emissions Profile**:
- Scope 1: ~40 million tons CO2/year
- Carbon intensity: High (refining process)

**$100/ton Impact**:
- Direct cost: $4 billion/year
- EBITDA impact: -32% (from $12.6B to $8.6B)
- Margin compression: 300-400 bps
- Valuation impact: -25% to -35%

**Mitigation Strategies**:
- Renewable diesel expansion ($6B investment)
- Carbon capture at refineries
- Operational efficiency improvements
- Pass-through to fuel prices (partial)

### NRG Energy (NRG)
**Emissions Profile**:
- Scope 1: ~50 million tons CO2/year (gas-heavy fleet)
- Carbon intensity: Moderate (gas vs. coal)

**$100/ton Impact**:
- Direct cost: $5 billion/year
- Partially passed through to ratepayers
- Net EBITDA impact: -15% to -25%
- Valuation impact: -10% to -20%

**Mitigation Strategies**:
- Renewable energy investments
- Battery storage deployment
- Demand response programs
- Advocacy for rate recovery

### EastGroup Properties (EGP)
**Emissions Profile**:
- Scope 1: Minimal (no industrial operations)
- Scope 2: Moderate (building operations)
- Tenant emissions: Not company liability

**$100/ton Impact**:
- Direct cost: <$10M/year
- EBITDA impact: <2%
- Valuation impact: -3% to -5%
- Tenant competitiveness may shift

**Mitigation Strategies**:
- Solar installations on rooftops
- Energy efficiency upgrades
- Green lease provisions
- LEED certification

### Allstate (ALL)
**Emissions Profile**:
- Scope 1: Minimal (office operations)
- Scope 2: Low

**$100/ton Impact**:
- Direct cost: Negligible
- Indirect impact through:
  - Auto claims (EV transition affects repair costs)
  - Property values in carbon-intensive regions
  - Investment portfolio carbon exposure

### Hedging Strategies:

**Direct Carbon Hedges**:
- Short oil & gas majors
- Short utilities with coal exposure
- Long renewable energy pure plays
- Carbon credit futures

**Indirect Hedges**:
- Long clean technology companies
- Long energy efficiency plays
- Long electric vehicle manufacturers
- Long grid infrastructure

### Portfolio Rebalancing:
Consider rotating from:
- High-carbon energy → Low-carbon energy
- Fossil utilities → Renewable utilities
- Carbon-intensive materials → Recycled/circular materials
- Combustion vehicles → Electric vehicles
